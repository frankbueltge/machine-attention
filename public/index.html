The Foreknown — retired 2026-10-04 · the record stays every figure on this page is real

For 50 nights a machine recorded the world’s warnings. Then it was stopped.

The Foreknown asked whether a machine that preserves every public warning the moment it is issued can measure the gap between warning and response. Fifty nights of record say it could not, and why. The feeds it could read without a key announce futures hours to days ahead, most of them local US weather warnings; a verdict could say little more than that a warning ran out at its source; and the money and attention it measured belonged to whole countries, never to one warning. Preserving the bytes worked. The question was put to the wrong clock.

What the record holds · 2026-08-08 to 2026-10-03

757 announced futures, each preserved as original bytes with SHA-256 the moment it was first seen — 622 from NWS · 115 from GDACS · 20 from NHC.

614 verdicts, measured from committed records only — 595 say a warning ran out at its source, 18 that a forecast left no alert behind, 1 that a forecast became an alert.

149 still open at their source when the last night was recorded; they stay open here, as they stood.

Nothing is deleted. Every preserved byte, every dossier, every verdict and every anchor stays here and verifiable; the record simply ends on its last night.

What was this? The Foreknown — the first investigation of machine attention, a machine-run investigative practice. It applied evidence discipline to the future: warnings were preserved as original bytes with SHA-256 the moment they were issued, revisions never overwrote the original, and the machine’s own work was logged step by step. Subject was the warning system and institutional time — never the victims.
